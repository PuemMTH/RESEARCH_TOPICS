\begin{itemize}
    \item Our method needs mentions to produce captions, but identifying the mentions for figures in real-world data is not always easy. 18.81\% of figures in original \oldDataset did not have any identified mentions, which we excluded in this work.
Many factors contributed to the gap, including
errors caused by upstream components such as image extraction or image type classification (\eg, table),
unexpected figure index formats (\eg, ``Figure VIII'', ``Figure C·1'',``Fig.Fig. 4(b)''),
PDF parsing errors, or 
the figure never being explicitly referred to in the paper. Our procedure is not applicable to such cases.

\item
Our paper focused on non-compound line charts in arXiv papers, and the human evaluation only focused on NLP papers.
More research is needed to examine the generalizability.

\item 
The proposed method uses texts instead of images as the primary information source, so it is not capable of capturing the visual content that only occurs in the image.

\item 
The quality of the generated caption in our approach is bounded by the text quality of the paragraph.
When the article is poorly written, our models will likely struggle.

\end{itemize}
